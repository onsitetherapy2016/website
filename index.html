Understanding compression

The science
behind the support.

Long periods of standing or sitting still limit the movement that helps circulation. Appropriately fitted compression can be part of a broader approach that includes regular movement.

Explore common questions
01

Support venous blood return

Graduated leg compression applies more pressure at the ankle and less farther up the leg, helping blood move back toward the heart.

Read the South Tees NHS guide
02

Work with the lymphatic system

Compression provides resistance for muscles to work against. In clinical lymphoedema care, compression combined with movement helps fluid drain from an affected limb.

Read the NHS guide to lymphoedema treatment
03

Keep movement in the workday

Build in opportunities to move and change position. Compression complements an appropriate routine; it does not replace movement or improvements to the workstation.

These sources explain compression therapy generally, not clinical testing of OST products. Ask a qualified healthcare professional whether compression is appropriate for you, especially if you have circulation problems.
